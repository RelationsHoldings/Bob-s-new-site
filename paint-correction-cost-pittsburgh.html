Paint correction is one of the most variable-priced services in detailing. The same car could need a $300 single-stage polish or a $1,200 three-stage compound-polish-finish job depending on paint condition. Here's how it actually breaks down.
What Affects Price

Key Pricing Factors

Number of stages
Single-stage correction (one polish pass) removes light swirls and oxidation. Multi-stage correction (compound, then polish, then finishing polish) tackles deeper RIDS, paint transfer, and serious neglect.
Vehicle size
Sedans take 4-8 hours per stage. SUVs and trucks take 6-12 hours per stage. Pricing scales with time on the vehicle.
Paint type & color
Black, dark blue, and red paints show defects most aggressively and often need extra polishing time. White, silver, and gray paints can sometimes get away with less.
Existing damage severity
Deep scratches, swirl marks from automatic car washes, and oxidation all require different levels of correction. The more abused the paint, the more time it takes.
Coating after correction
Most clients pair correction with ceramic coating or sealant to lock in the result. This is an add-on cost but extends the value of the correction by years.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if I need paint correction?
Look at your paint in direct sunlight. If you see circular swirl marks, spiderweb patterns, or hazy reflections, you need correction. If your paint is glassy-smooth and reflects clearly, you don't.
Will paint correction remove every scratch?
It removes anything in the clear coat. Deep scratches that have gone through the clear coat into the base color need touch-up paint, not correction. We'll inspect and tell you what's realistic.
Is paint correction safe for my clear coat?
Yes, when done properly. We use paint-thickness gauges to monitor how much clear coat is being removed and stop before causing damage. Cheap or inexperienced correction can damage paint; our process is conservative.
Should I get correction or just touch-up scratches?
Correction works for swirls and oxidation across the whole paint surface. Touch-up paint works for deep individual scratches. Many cars need both: touch-up for the deep one-off scratches, then a single-stage correction to even everything out.
How long does paint correction take?
Single-stage on a sedan: 4-6 hours. Multi-stage on an SUV: 10-14+ hours. We sometimes do correction over two days to do it right.
